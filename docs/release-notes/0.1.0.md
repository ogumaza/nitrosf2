First release of nitrosf2, a MIDI, SoundFont 2 and SFZ converter for Nintendo DS games. It plays each sequence on a model of the game's sound driver and records the notes and controllers that the driver plays. Each sequence gets a MIDI file and a SoundFont containing its instruments. SFZ files are written beside them.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. Drop one or more cartridge images (`.nds`) or sound archives (`.sdat`) onto `nitrosf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `nitrosf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Nitrosf2.app`. Drop images onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## What it does

- **Input.** Reads DS and DSi cartridge images and loose sound archives. An image can hold several sound archives, in any of its files.
- **The SDK's sound driver.** Converts every sequence of the sound archives (SDAT) that nearly every DS game uses, on a model of the SDK's driver. The image's ARM7 program gives the driver's build.
- **Procyon Studio's driver (DSE).** Converts the songs and sound effects of Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Time, Explorers of Darkness and Explorers of Sky.
- **Universomo's driver.** Converts the 147 songs of Beat City: the 66 pieces of music and the 81 sound effects. The title screen's music is named `au105_title_screen`.
- **Game profiles.** Seven games, or families of games, have a profile of what the game's code does to their sequences: variables, channels, reverb and the tracks of each area. A sequence that the game plays in more than one way gets a file for each way.
- **MIDI.** The driver's tracks become MIDI tracks. Volume, pan, pitch bend, modulation and the reverb send become controllers. The main loop is marked for players that support loop markers.
- **SoundFont.** The samples, tuning, loops and envelopes of each instrument, at the levels of the driver. The player tested is FluidSynth 2.6.2. Its known departures from the specification are 0.4 of each zone's initialAttenuation, CC10 at half its specified amount, a 96 dB envelope range and slower releases under 15.6 ms. The SoundFonts allow for the first two. Every player in common use shares them. A player that follows the specification plays them quieter and with narrower pans.
- **SFZ.** One file per MIDI track, with WAV samples that all sequences of a folder share. The player tested is sfizz 1.2.3.
- **Reports.** Lists the approximations that MIDI, SoundFont or SFZ require for each sequence.

## Known limitations

- Some SDAT sequences convert about 2 dB quieter than the driver plays them. The renders were made in FluidSynth 2.6.2, whose known departures are 0.4 of each zone's initialAttenuation, CC10 at half its specified amount, a 96 dB envelope range and slower releases under 15.6 ms. The cause is still open.
- SoundFont players interpolate between a sample's points, and the DS doesn't. Samples with a lot of high frequencies play a little quieter in a SoundFont player. Beat City's music plays close to the game's level. A drum sample can play up to about 2 dB quieter in FluidSynth 2.6.2. The SFZ files play without interpolation.
- The console's reverb is left to the player. CC91 sets the send where a game starts it.
- Fades, and other changes that the game's code makes during play, stay out unless a profile lists them.
- A game that plays its music through another driver, or only as streamed recordings, converts nothing yet.
- See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
