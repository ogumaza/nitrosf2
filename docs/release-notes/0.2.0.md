nitrosf2 0.2.0 converts the songs of many more games on Procyon Studio's sound driver, Digital Sound Elements (DSE). It also fixes a crash and a loop that ended early in sequences of the SDK's sound archives, the speed of DSE instruments' LFOs, and SFZ samples that overwrote one another.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later, with no additional runtime DLLs. Drop one or more cartridge images (`.nds`) or sound archives (`.sdat`) onto `nitrosf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `nitrosf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Nitrosf2.app`. Drop images onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## New

- **DSE's builds.** nitrosf2 reads the game's code to tell which build of DSE's driver it runs, and plays each song on a model of that build. These games now convert, beside the three Explorers games:
  - Professor Layton and the Diabolical Box, Professor Layton and the Unwound Future, and Professor Layton and the Last Specter, with its London Life
  - Soma Bringer
  - Luminous Arc 2, and Luminous Arc 3: Eyes
  - Inazuma Eleven, Inazuma Eleven 2: Kyoui no Shinryakusha Fire, and Inazuma Eleven 3: Sekai e no Chousen!! Bomber and The Ogre
  - Fushigi no Dungeon: Fuurai no Shiren DS 2: Sabaku no Majou, Fuurai no Shiren 4: Kami no Hitomi to Akuma no Heso, and Fuurai no Shiren 5: Fortune Tower to Unmei no Dice
  - Ni no Kuni: Shikkoku no Madoushi. The game never plays the sequenced pieces in its files. Its music is streamed recordings.
- **The driver's tick.** DSE's driver steps its songs on a timer that the game sets. nitrosf2 plays each song at its game's rate: every 10 ms in most games, and once a video frame in the main game of Professor Layton and the Last Specter.
- **Songs in packs.** nitrosf2 finds DSE's songs and sound effects in the file system and inside the games' packs, compressed with the SDK's LZ77 or in Level-5's GFSA archives.
- **`--dse-model earlier|later`.** Converts the DSE songs and sound effects of a game whose build nitrosf2 doesn't model yet, on the model of Explorers of Time (earlier) or Explorers of Sky (later). The result only approximates the game.
- **Silent sequences.** A sequence that the sound archive gives a volume of 0 converts silent, because the game plays it silent too. The report says so for each such sequence.
- **Spirit Tracks' music manager.** The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks plays some songs in place of others, at levels that its mixer sets, and starts some songs at a section that a variable selects. nitrosf2 converts those songs as the game plays them. Where the game plays a song two ways, a second file ends in `_INTRO` or `_RESUME`.

## Fixes

- nitrosf2 no longer closes partway through an image at a tied note whose pitch sweep has run out.
- A note struck too quietly to sound no longer counts as sounding. A sequence that ends in such notes now takes its loop as the game plays it.
- DSE's driver runs each phase of an instrument's LFO in whole steps of the game's timer. The SoundFonts and SFZ files now do the same. The LFOs' speed changes a little in the Explorers games and in several of the newly supported games. The MIDI files are unchanged.
- In a DSE folder, two wave banks can share an id. Their SFZ samples had the same file names, and one bank's samples replaced the other's. The SFZ files now name such samples after the banks' files. The sample file names change wherever two banks in a folder share an id. That includes the Explorers games, the games that 0.1.0 already converted. The SoundFonts and MIDI files are unchanged.

## Known limitations

- Apart from the Explorers games, most DSE games keep their sound effects in a layout of bank that nitrosf2 doesn't read yet. Ni no Kuni's sound effects are among them. nitrosf2 converts such a game's songs only, and gives a warning for each such bank.
- Luminous Arc keeps its DSE files in version 0x402. nitrosf2 doesn't find DSE files of that version yet. Nor does it find songs in packs compressed other than with the SDK's LZ77 or in Level-5's GFSA archives. Such an image gives no warning about them.
- Some SDAT sequences convert about 2 dB quieter than the driver plays them. The renders were made in FluidSynth 2.6.2, whose known departures are 0.4 of each zone's initialAttenuation, CC10 at half its specified amount, a 96 dB envelope range and slower releases under 15.6 ms. The cause is still open.
- SoundFont players interpolate between a sample's points, and the DS doesn't. Samples with a lot of high frequencies play a little quieter in a SoundFont player. The SFZ files play without interpolation.
- The console's reverb is left to the player. CC91 sets the send where a game starts it.
- Fades, and other changes that the game's code makes during play, stay out unless a profile lists them.
- A game that plays its music through another driver, or only as streamed recordings, converts nothing yet.
- See "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
